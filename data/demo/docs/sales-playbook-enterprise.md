# Enterprise Sales Playbook

For deals of 50+ robots or any customer that needs Atlas Enterprise (SSO). Owner: Lena Fischer.

## Ideal customer

- 3PLs, retail and grocery distribution with 2+ sites.
- Labor-constrained picking operations running 2-3 shifts.
- An IT team that will want SSO, audit logs and security review.

## Deal stages

1. **Discovery** (Lena): site count, SKU mix, Wi-Fi environment, current WMS, IT/security requirements.
2. **Site assessment** (Support Engineering, usually Marco Rossi): Wi-Fi survey and floor walk. Flag sites without 802.11r or with mixed-vendor APs.
3. **Pilot** (4-6 weeks, 5-10 robots): success criteria written down before shipping.
4. **Proposal**: price from the Pricing doc; volume and multi-year discounts only.
5. **Security review**: send the security questionnaire pack; loop in Devon Lee for SSO/SAML questions.
6. **Close and handoff** to the CSM (Omar Haddad) with a written handoff note.

## Positioning Atlas

- Atlas replaces the legacy Cloud Console with multi-site fleet visibility, scheduling and OTA updates.
- **SSO is Enterprise tier only** ($55/robot/month). If a prospect's IT requires SAML, quote Enterprise from day one.
- Never commit an Atlas feature date in writing without Sam Rivera's sign-off. Check #atlas-launch for the current GA date.

## Common objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Robots are too expensive" | Lease at $1,450/month; payback typically under 14 months vs. temp labor. |
| "Our Wi-Fi is patchy" | Site assessment is free; we tune roaming per site. |
| "We need SSO before rollout" | Enterprise tier with KeyGate-backed SAML; confirm timeline with Product. |
| "Billing is complex" | Usage-based invoicing via PayStream; Mei Chen can join a call for billing questions. |

## Rules of engagement

- Log every enterprise deal in the pipeline review in #sales weekly.
- Discounts above 15% need Elena Park's approval.
- Customer escalations about product dates go to Sam Rivera and the CSM the same day.
